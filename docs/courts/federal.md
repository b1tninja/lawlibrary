The federal judicial hierarchy under Article III, plus Article I courts and bankruptcy units when an official page or the U.S. Code says so. Checked 2026-09-24 against [uscourts.gov](https://www.uscourts.gov/), [supremecourt.gov](https://www.supremecourt.gov/), and [uscode.house.gov](https://uscode.house.gov/). ## Article III levels

Article III of the Constitution establishes the federal judiciary. The Administrative Office’s [Court Role and Structure](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure) page states that Article III created the Supreme Court and authorized Congress to establish lower courts. The educational [About the Supreme Court](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/educational-resources/about-educational-outreach/activity-resources/about) page quotes Article III, Section 1: the judicial power is vested in one Supreme Court and in such inferior courts as Congress may ordain and establish. The same page summarizes Article III, Section 2 on original and appellate jurisdiction.

## Bankruptcy courts (units of the district courts)

Bankruptcy courts are **not** a separate Article III tier. [About U.S. Bankruptcy Courts](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure/about-us-bankruptcy-courts) states they are units of the district courts that hear bankruptcy cases and proceedings referred by the district courts. [28 U.S.C. § 151](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title28-section151&num=0&edition=prelim) designates that unit as the bankruptcy court for the district. Federal courts have exclusive jurisdiction over bankruptcy cases. Appeals go to the district court, to a bankruptcy appellate panel where a circuit has one, or to the court of appeals when statute allows ([About U.S. Bankruptcy Courts](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure/about-us-bankruptcy-courts)).

## Article I and territorial courts

Congress created several Article I courts that are not part of the Third Branch. The [Court Role and Structure](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure) page lists, among others, the U.S. Tax Court (and also the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims and the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces). Include a court here only when an official page or the Code says Article I.

### Territorial courts

[Types of Federal Judges](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/types-federal-judges) states that Congress established three territorial courts—in the U.S. Virgin Islands, the Northern Mariana Islands, and Guam—with judges appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate to renewable **10-year** terms. [About U.S. District Courts](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/court-role-and-structure/about-us-district-courts) states that all but three districts have lifetime-appointed Article III judges, and that judges serve 10-year terms in those three territorial districts. Those pages do **not** use the words “Article I” for the territorial courts; this note therefore does not label them Article I. The same district-courts page also notes that Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands, Guam, and the Northern Mariana Islands have courts that hear federal cases (including bankruptcy); Puerto Rico is listed among the First Circuit’s composition in [28 U.S.C. § 41](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:28%20section:41%20edition:prelim)), separate from the three 10-year territorial courts named above.
