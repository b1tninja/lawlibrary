# California judicial branch

The California Constitution, code `CONS` in the local Whoosh index, places the judiciary in article VI. Section numbers collide across articles, so every hit below was kept only when its article heading contains `ARTICLE VI JUDICIAL`.

Official branch portals: [courts.ca.gov](https://www.courts.ca.gov/), [courts.ca.gov/courts](https://www.courts.ca.gov/courts) (three tiers), and [supreme.courts.ca.gov](https://supreme.courts.ca.gov/).

## Constitutional structure (article VI)

| Section | What it does | Index text (article heading `ARTICLE VI JUDICIAL`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SEC. 1 | Vests the judicial power | “The judicial power of this State is vested in the Supreme Court, courts of appeal, and superior courts, all of which are courts of record.” |
| SEC. 2 | Supreme Court membership | “The Supreme Court consists of the Chief Justice of California and 6 associate justices.” |
| SEC. 3 | Courts of appeal districts | “The Legislature shall divide the State into districts each containing a court of appeal with one or more divisions.” |
| SEC. 4 | One superior court per county; appellate division | “In each county there is a superior court of one or more judges.” … “In each superior court there is an appellate division.” |
| SEC. 6 | Judicial Council | Creates the council and directs it, among other duties, to “adopt rules for court administration, practice and procedure” (not inconsistent with statute). |
| SEC. 10 | Original jurisdiction | Habeas corpus and extraordinary writs (mandamus, certiorari, prohibition); superior courts have original jurisdiction in all other causes. |
| SEC. 11 | Appellate jurisdiction | Supreme Court has appellate jurisdiction when judgment of death has been pronounced; otherwise courts of appeal (and, as statute provides, the superior court appellate division) hear appeals from superior courts. |

## Supreme Court

Article VI, section 2 fixes the court at the Chief Justice and six associate justices. Government Code chapter 3 is titled `CHAPTER 3. The Supreme Court [68801. - 68933.]`. Within that chapter, article 3 (`The Reporter of Decisions of the Supreme Court and the Courts of Appeal [68900. - 68905.]`) has the Supreme Court appoint the Reporter (GOV 68900) and publish selected opinions in the official reports under the court’s general supervision (GOV 68902).

Official site: [supreme.courts.ca.gov](https://supreme.courts.ca.gov/). Opinions page: [supreme.courts.ca.gov/opinions](https://supreme.courts.ca.gov/opinions).

## Courts of Appeal — six districts

Article VI, section 3 leaves district lines to the Legislature. The judicial branch’s Courts of Appeal home lists the six districts and the counties each serves ([appellate.courts.ca.gov](https://appellate.courts.ca.gov/)). The same county lists appear on [courts.ca.gov](https://courts.ca.gov/) Courts of Appeal pages.

## Superior courts

Article VI, section 4 puts one superior court in each county, with an appellate division. There are 58 superior (trial) courts, one per county ([courts.ca.gov/courts](https://www.courts.ca.gov/courts)). Government Code chapter 5 is titled `CHAPTER 5. The Superior Courts [69504. - 70141.11.]`.

## Judicial Council and the California Rules of Court

Article VI, section 6 creates the Judicial Council. The official Judicial Council page states that it is “the administrative and policymaking body of the California courts,” and that under the Chief Justice and the California Constitution it is “responsible for ensuring the consistent, independent, impartial, and accessible administration of justice” ([courts.ca.gov/policy-administration/judicial-council](https://www.courts.ca.gov/policy-administration/judicial-council)).

In the index, Government Code chapter 2 is `CHAPTER 2. The Judicial Council [68500. - 68641.]` (beginning around GOV 68500).

The California Rules of Court are published on the judicial branch site: [courts.ca.gov/forms-rules/rules-court](https://www.courts.ca.gov/forms-rules/rules-court). California Rules of Court, rule 1.3, states that the rules are adopted by the Judicial Council under article VI, section 6 (except specified titles adopted by the Supreme Court).

## Reporter of Decisions and the California Style Manual

GOV 68900–68905 appoint and fund the Reporter of Decisions and govern official-report publication. GOV 68902: opinions the Supreme Court deems expedient “shall be published in the official reports” under the Supreme Court’s general supervision.

The fourth edition of the *California Style Manual* (Edward W. Jessen, Reporter of Decisions) carries Supreme Court of California copyright and a December 21, 1999 Supreme Court approval adopting it, pursuant to Government Code section 68902, “as the official organ for the styles to be used in the publication of the Official Reports.” It is therefore an official Supreme Court / Reporter-of-Decisions style organ for the Official Reports, not a free-standing commercial citation product.

There is no dedicated official hosting URL for the full Style Manual on `courts.ca.gov` or `supreme.courts.ca.gov`. The Rules of Court cite it (for example rule 1.200 on citation format; rule 8.204 encourages the fourth edition for briefs) but do not publish the manual text. Commercial and third-party PDFs exist; they are not recorded here as an official branch URL.

**Index note:** a search of the local Whoosh index for “California Style Manual” returns no statute or constitution section titled *California Style Manual*.

## Official opinion sites

| What | Official URL |
| --- | --- |
| Supreme Court opinions (final opinions and forthcoming notices) | [supreme.courts.ca.gov/opinions](https://supreme.courts.ca.gov/opinions) |
| Slip opinions (Supreme Court and Courts of Appeal, recent filings) | [courts.ca.gov/cms/opinions.htm](https://www.courts.ca.gov/cms/opinions.htm) |
| Published / citable Official Reports (searchable; LexisNexis no-fee service linked from the branch) | Linked from the published-opinions page above as Official Reports Opinions (1850–present) |

The published-opinions page distinguishes Official Reports opinions (citable, with post-filing corrections) from slip opinions (as-filed versions for the last 120 days). Supreme Court slip opinions post at filing on Mondays and Thursdays at 10:00 a.m.; Court of Appeal slip opinions post throughout the day after filing.
