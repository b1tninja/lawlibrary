## What they require

Title 1, United States Code, section 104 says each section shall be numbered, and shall contain, as nearly as may be, a single proposition of enactment. The House guide quotes that sentence and calls the section the basic unit of a bill and of an enacted statute.

The 2022 House manual says a further subdivision must nest inside the preceding subdivision. A subsection that has both paragraphs and subparagraphs must put the subparagraphs inside the paragraphs. A unique section number is best practice. The same manual says an omnibus bill may repeat a section number, and that this is acceptable. The order it prints under a section is `(a)` subsection, `(1)` paragraph, `(A)` subparagraph, `(i)` clause, `(I)` subclause, `(aa)` item, `(AA)` subitem.

California does not use the word subsection for `(a)`. Public Utilities Code section 10 says "section" means a section of that code unless some other statute is specifically mentioned, and "subdivision" means a subdivision of the section in which that word occurs unless some other section is expressly mentioned. `muster` uses those California words: subdivision, paragraph, subparagraph, clause.

Welfare and Institutions Code section 6 says division, part, chapter, article, and section headings shall not be deemed to govern, limit, modify, or affect the scope, meaning, or intent of the provisions. A heading is a label. It is not the operative sentence.

Joint Rule 8.5 says a bill may not be introduced unless the Legislative Counsel has attached the cover and a digest. It does not say the letters must be consecutive.

Rule 1.200 says citations in a document filed in the courts must follow the California Style Manual or The Bluebook, consistently. That governs the brief.

No source in this list says a skipped `(b)` is void. A skip remains a reason to look again. It may be a repeal, a decimal insertion, a parser miss, or the text.
